**The entry: one letter, `M`. The word behind it: MAYHEM.**

## Why M

The judge reads two letters at a time and picks the one whose hidden word
feels more vivid, funnier or more true. M wins that duel:

- **Vivid and specific.** Mayhem is a sharp picture, not a vague mood:
  everything happening at once, loud and fast — twenty-four free bounties
  raced in a single day, builders running in parallel, deadlines chased one
  after another.
- **Funny at once.** "M for mayhem" lands in a second and is the kind of
  line a reader repeats to a friend.
- **True.** It is an honest report of a real day, ordinary in its chaos.
- **The letter fits.** M is mayhem's own initial — the sharper M, not the
  obvious "messy", "magical" or "Monday". And M's other natural day-word,
  "manic", is just as strong, so whichever word the judge infers behind
  the letter, M still wins most duels.
- **Not the brief's examples.** A for amazing, F for Friday-feeling and Z
  for zero sleep are the obvious picks the brief itself hands out — the
  field is full of them. Z alone drew 151 titled entries; M drew 15.

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) writes exactly one byte, `M`,
to `/output/entry.txt` — no word, no gloss, no punctuation, no trailing
newline. The sealed gate requires exactly 1 character of UTF-8 text.

```sh
node main.ts            # writes /output/entry.txt (or $OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt)
```
